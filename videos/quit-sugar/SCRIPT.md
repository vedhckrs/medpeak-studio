## Sources

- **who-diet** World Health Organization. Healthy diet (fact sheet). 'free sugars should be limited to less than 10% of total daily energy intake, which is equivalent to 50 g (or about 12 level teaspoons) for a person of healthy body weight consuming about 2000 calories per day'; free sugars include sugars 'added to foods and beverages by the manufacturer, cook or consumer, and sugars naturally present in honey, syrups, fruit juices and fruit juice concentrates'; 'Reducing consumption of free sugars ... should be accomplished without the use of non-sugar sweeteners.' <https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet>
- **temorenga-2013** Te Morenga L, Mallard S, Mann J. Dietary sugars and body weight: systematic review and meta-analyses of randomised controlled trials and cohort studies. BMJ 2013;346:e7492. 'In trials of adults with ad libitum diets ... reduced intake of dietary sugars was associated with a decrease in body weight (0.80 kg, 95% CI 0.39 to 1.21)'; 'Isoenergetic exchange of dietary sugars with other carbohydrates showed no change in body weight'; the change 'seems to be mediated via changes in energy intakes'. The video rounds 0.8 kg to 'about one kilo'. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23321486/>
- **schwarz-2017** Schwarz JM et al. Effects of dietary fructose restriction on liver fat, de novo lipogenesis, and insulin kinetics in children with obesity. Gastroenterology 2017;153:743-52. 41 children aged 9-18 with obesity, 9 days, the same calories with starch in place of sugar: 'liver fat decreased from a median of 7.2% ... to 3.8%'; 'insulin kinetics improved'. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28579536/>
- **wise-2016** Wise PM, Nattress L, Flammer LJ, Beauchamp GK. Reduced dietary intake of simple sugars alters perceived sweet taste intensity but not perceived pleasantness. Am J Clin Nutr 2016;103:50-60. Low-sugar group 16 (13 completed key parts), control 17: 'no systematic differences ... during the baseline or first diet month'; 'During the third diet month, the low-sugar subjects rated both low and high concentrations in puddings as ∼40% sweeter than did the control group'. One small trial; two authors worked at PepsiCo (declared). <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26607941/>
- **westwater-2016** Westwater ML, Fletcher PC, Ziauddeen H. Sugar addiction: the state of the science. Eur J Nutr 2016;55(Suppl 2):55-69. 'We find little evidence to support sugar addiction in humans'. No conflicts. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27372453/>
- **moynihan-2014** Moynihan PJ, Kelly SA. Effect on caries of restricting sugars intake: systematic review to inform WHO guidelines. J Dent Res 2014;93:8-18. 55 studies; 'evidence of moderate quality showing that caries is lower when free-sugars intake is < 10% E'. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24323509/>
