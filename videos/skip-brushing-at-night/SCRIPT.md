## Sources

- **nhs-teeth** NHS. How to keep your teeth clean (page last reviewed 30 June 2025). 'Brush your teeth twice a day for about 2 minutes each time. Brush them last thing before you go to bed, as night-time brushing is important, and on 1 other occasion.' 'After brushing, spit out any excess toothpaste. Don't rinse your mouth immediately after brushing, as it'll wash away the concentrated fluoride in the remaining toothpaste.' <https://www.nhs.uk/live-well/healthy-teeth-and-gums/how-to-keep-your-teeth-clean/>
- **dawes-2008** Dawes C. Salivary flow patterns and the health of hard and soft oral tissues. J Am Dent Assoc. 2008 (PMID 18460676). 'Saliva protects against dental caries'. 'The most important time for toothbrushing is just before bedtime, because salivary flow is negligible during sleep and the protective effects of saliva are lost.' A review by one author, not a systematic review. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18460676/>
- **nidcr** NIH National Institute of Dental and Craniofacial Research. The Tooth Decay Process: How to Reverse It and Avoid a Cavity (reviewed January 2025). 'Whenever we eat or drink something that contains sugar or starch, the bacteria use them to produce acids. These acids begin to eat away at the tooth's hard outer surface, or enamel.' 'Our saliva can help fight off this acid attack.' 'A white spot may appear where minerals have been lost. This is a sign of early decay.' <https://www.nidcr.nih.gov/health-info/tooth-decay/more-info/tooth-decay-process>
- **nidcr-decay** NIH National Institute of Dental and Craniofacial Research. Tooth Decay (reviewed September 2025). 'In early tooth decay, there are not usually any symptoms. As tooth decay advances, it can cause a toothache (tooth pain) or tooth sensitivity to sweets, hot, or cold.' <https://www.nidcr.nih.gov/health-info/tooth-decay>
