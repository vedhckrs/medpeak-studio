## Description

What happens when you rub your eyes? Hands carry germs, so always wash your hands before touching your eyes (NEI). Rubbing hard and often is a known problem: reviews of the research say vigorous, prolonged rubbing may thin the clear front of the eye (the cornea) and lead to keratoconus, a change in its shape, and that the habit should be controlled, especially in children and people with eye allergies (PubMed reviews, 2009 and 2019). For itchy eyes, the NHS advises not rubbing them and holding a cold flannel on them for a few minutes to cool them down. Ask for an urgent appointment (NHS 111 in the UK) if you have eye pain, sensitivity to light, changes in your vision or very red eyes; for a chemical in the eye, a sharp object, a change in sight after an injury or severe pain, go to A&E or call your emergency number (999 in the UK, 911 in the US, 112 in the EU).
