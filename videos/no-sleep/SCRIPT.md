## Sources

- **xie-2013** Xie L et al. Sleep drives metabolite clearance from the adult brain. Science 2013;342:373-7. In live mice, 'natural sleep or anesthesia are associated with a 60% increase in the interstitial space', with a 'striking increase in convective exchange of cerebrospinal fluid with interstitial fluid', which 'increased the rate of β-amyloid clearance during sleep'. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24136970/>
- **shokri-2018** Shokri-Kojori E et al. (NIH). β-Amyloid accumulation in the human brain after one night of sleep deprivation. PNAS 2018;115:4483-8. In 20 healthy people, 'one night of sleep deprivation, relative to baseline, resulted in a significant increase in Aβ burden in the right hippocampus and thalamus' (PET scans). It does not show disease. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29632177/>
- **williamson-2000** Williamson AM, Feyer AM. Moderate sleep deprivation produces impairments ... equivalent to legally prescribed levels of alcohol intoxication. Occup Environ Med 2000;57:649-55. 'After 17-19 hours without sleep ... performance on some tests was equivalent or worse than that at a BAC of 0.05%.' 39 subjects. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10984335/>
- **nhlbi-effects** NHLBI (NIH). Sleep Deprivation and Deficiency: How Sleep Affects Your Health (updated 15 June 2022). Microsleep is 'brief moments of sleep that happen when you're normally awake'; 'You can't control microsleep, and you might not be aware of it'; sleep deficiency 'harms your driving ability as much or more than being drunk'. <https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/sleep-deprivation/health-effects>
- **nhlbi-hours** NHLBI (NIH). Sleep Deprivation and Deficiency: How Much Sleep Is Enough (updated 24 March 2022). Adults 18 or older: '7 to 8 hours a day'. 'If you regularly lose sleep or choose to sleep less than needed, the sleep loss adds up.' <https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/sleep-deprivation/how-much-sleep>
- **cdc-sleep** CDC. About Sleep (15 May 2024). Recommended sleep for adults 18-60 years: '7 or more hours'; 61-64 years: 7-9 hours; 65 and older: 7-8 hours. <https://www.cdc.gov/sleep/about/index.html>
