## Sources

- **nidcr** NIH National Institute of Dental and Craniofacial Research. The Tooth Decay Process: How to Reverse It and Avoid a Cavity (reviewed January 2025). 'Whenever we eat or drink something that contains sugar or starch, the bacteria use them to produce acids. These acids begin to eat away at the tooth's hard outer surface, or enamel.' 'Our saliva can help fight off this acid attack.' 'Enamel can repair itself by using minerals from saliva, and fluoride from toothpaste or other sources.' 'Limit between-meal snacks. This reduces the number of acid attacks on teeth and gives teeth a chance to repair themselves.' 'Brushing with fluoride toothpaste two times each day is important for preventing cavities.' 'A white spot may appear where minerals have been lost. This is a sign of early decay.' 'Over time, the enamel is weakened and destroyed, forming a cavity. A cavity is permanent damage that a dentist has to repair with a filling.' <https://www.nidcr.nih.gov/health-info/tooth-decay/more-info/tooth-decay-process>
- **who-sugars** World Health Organization. Sugars and dental caries, fact sheet (14 August 2025). 'Dental caries results when plaque forms on the surface of a tooth and converts the free sugars ... into acids that destroy the tooth over time.' WHO advises limiting free sugars 'to less than 10% of total energy intake - and ideally to less than 5%'. <https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/sugars-and-dental-caries>
- **nidcr-decay** NIH National Institute of Dental and Craniofacial Research. Tooth Decay (reviewed September 2025). 'In early tooth decay, there are not usually any symptoms. As tooth decay advances, it can cause a toothache (tooth pain) or tooth sensitivity to sweets, hot, or cold.' 'This can lead to a small hole in a tooth, called a cavity.' <https://www.nidcr.nih.gov/health-info/tooth-decay>
