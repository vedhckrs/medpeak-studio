## The idea in one line

Dad quits sugar, and a cheeky sugar cube called **Sweetie** fights to win him back for 30 days. Every
few days Sweetie tries a new trick (cravings, juice, diet soda), and each time a part of Dad's body
answers it with what the studies really found. Day 30 ends with a surprise: the tongue is not done yet.

- **Story shape: `fight`** (two sides battle). The last three were `clock` (rabies), `crew` (no-sleep)
  and `journey` (creatine), so this is new on the new look. No Dr. Mira: the body parts answer.
- **The clock on screen is a wall calendar** in Dad's kitchen. Dad rips off a page at each round
  (Day 1, Day 3, Day 7, Day 10, Day 20, Day 30). The page flying is the cut to the next round.
- **About 48 s**, then Joey's CTA end card.
- **Places (4):** the kitchen at night (new view and colours: rabies used a daytime kitchen), the brain
  (the no-sleep brain place, a new view), inside the mouth (new, used twice: tooth, then tongue), inside
  the liver (new).

Lines written with care:
- **D1 and D2** say "kids with obesity", because that is who the study was in. "Nearly halved" is
  7.2% down to 3.8%. Never "reverses fatty liver".
- **F2**: the BMJ review found 0.80 kg in adults eating freely, and no change when sugar was swapped for
  the same calories of other carbs. So "only because you ate less" is the paper's own conclusion.
  "About one kilo" rounds 0.8 up; if you want it exact, the line becomes "under a kilo".
- **G2** is from one small trial (13 people finished the low-sugar part), and the effect came in month
  two and three, not in the first month. That is why the tongue says "not yet" at Day 30. The
  description says "one small trial".
- **C3**: WHO's limit of 12 level teaspoons is for a person eating about 2,000 calories a day. The
  on-screen tag says "about 2,000 calories a day".

## Care (the topic bank's rule: no cure or detox claims)

- **No cure, detox, "reverses", "melts fat" or "you will lose X kg".** The weight line is small and
  explained.
- **"Quit sugar" means free sugars**: added sugar, honey, syrups and juice (WHO's own list). Whole fruit
  is not on that list; if you want, a tag on C2 can say "whole fruit is fine".
- **Studies in children stay "in kids"** (D1, D2).
- **Addiction**: Westwater 2016 (Cambridge, no conflicts) found "little evidence to support sugar
  addiction in humans". B2 says only that. It does not say cravings are not real.
- **Industry tie, for you to know**: two authors of the taste trial (wise-2016) worked at PepsiCo.
  It is stated in the paper. It is the only trial on this question I found, so I kept it and the line
  says what it measured, nothing more.

## Sources (every quote read on 2026-11-22)

- **who-diet** WHO. Healthy diet, fact sheet. https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/healthy-diet.
  "free sugars should be limited to less than 10% of total daily energy intake, which is equivalent to 50 g
  (or about 12 level teaspoons) for a person of healthy body weight consuming about 2000 calories per day";
  free sugars include sugars "added to foods and beverages by the manufacturer, cook or consumer, and sugars
  naturally present in honey, syrups, fruit juices and fruit juice concentrates"; "Reducing consumption of
  free sugars ... should be accomplished without the use of non-sugar sweeteners."
- **temorenga-2013** Te Morenga L, Mallard S, Mann J. Dietary sugars and body weight: systematic review and
  meta-analyses of randomised controlled trials and cohort studies. BMJ 2013;346:e7492. PubMed 23321486.
  "In trials of adults with ad libitum diets ... reduced intake of dietary sugars was associated with a
  decrease in body weight (0.80 kg, 95% CI 0.39 to 1.21)"; "Isoenergetic exchange of dietary sugars with
  other carbohydrates showed no change in body weight"; the change "seems to be mediated via changes in
  energy intakes".
- **schwarz-2017** Schwarz JM et al. Effects of dietary fructose restriction on liver fat, de novo
  lipogenesis, and insulin kinetics in children with obesity. Gastroenterology 2017;153:743-52. PubMed
  28579536. 41 children aged 9-18, 9 days, same calories, starch in place of sugar; "liver fat decreased
  from a median of 7.2% ... to 3.8%"; "insulin kinetics improved".
- **wise-2016** Wise PM, Nattress L, Flammer LJ, Beauchamp GK. Reduced dietary intake of simple sugars
  alters perceived sweet taste intensity but not perceived pleasantness. Am J Clin Nutr 2016;103:50-60.
  PubMed 26607941. Low-sugar group 16 (13 completed key parts), control 17; "no systematic differences
  ... during the baseline or first diet month"; "During the third diet month, the low-sugar subjects
  rated both low and high concentrations in puddings as ∼40% sweeter than did the control group".
- **westwater-2016** Westwater ML, Fletcher PC, Ziauddeen H. Sugar addiction: the state of the science.
  Eur J Nutr 2016;55(Suppl 2):55-69. PubMed 27372453. "We find little evidence to support sugar addiction
  in humans". No conflicts.
- **moynihan-2014** Moynihan PJ, Kelly SA. Effect on caries of restricting sugars intake: systematic review
  to inform WHO guidelines. J Dent Res 2014;93:8-18. PubMed 24323509. 55 studies; "evidence of moderate
  quality showing that caries is lower when free-sugars intake is < 10% E".

Read but not used: Lustig 2016 (PubMed 26499447, the same children's study, blood pressure and blood fats
after 9 days). It would add a sixth fact; the script already has enough.
