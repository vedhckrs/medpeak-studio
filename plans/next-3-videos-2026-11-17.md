## Video 2: What Happens If You Swallow Gum (the journey)

**The idea:** we follow one piece of gum all the way through, like a lost tourist.

1. **First frame:** a pink gum with a face and a tiny suitcase, falling down the food pipe.
   "You swallow gum. Seven years inside you?"
2. **The stomach:** the acid crew sprays it, scrubs it, gives up. It is still there. The stomach
   mixes it with dinner like everything else.
3. **The exit:** between meals the stomach sweeps out what is left, and the gum is pushed
   through the door at the bottom.
4. **The gut:** a long winding tunnel. The gum rides along with the leftovers. A small route map
   in the corner shows how far it has come.
5. **Dr. Mira, 6 to 8 seconds:** a swallowed thing that reaches the stomach will most likely go
   all the way through, often within a week. But doctors reported three children with a
   blockage from swallowed gum, so gum is for chewing.
6. **Last frame:** the gum waves goodbye at the end of the tunnel. "Not seven years!"

**Three places** instead of one: food pipe, stomach, gut.
**Honest limit (unchanged):** no allowed source names gum or tests "seven years". The script
answers with what the US National Library of Medicine says about swallowed objects and never
says the stomach "cannot digest" gum. The acid crew "giving up" is a joke I must word carefully
or drop: I will show you the exact line.

## Video 3: What Happens When You Burp (the crew at work)

**The idea:** the top of the stomach is a room with a door, and a doorman who keeps it shut.

1. **First frame:** a crowd of air bubbles with faces squashed against a door, the doorman
   leaning on it with all his weight. "When you burp, a tiny door opens."
2. **Earlier:** a huge pour of fizzy drink comes in from outside. With every gulp more bubbles
   ride down and join the crowd.
3. **The squeeze:** the room stretches. The doorman sweats. "Guess how many times a day you burp?"
4. **The burp:** the stretch is the signal. He opens the door, the crowd rushes up and out.
5. **One line at home:** the boy at the kitchen table. "Burp! Oops. Excuse me!"
6. **The fix and the answer, from the stomach:** a burp is air you swallowed, not your food.
   Eat slowly, go easy on fizzy drinks and gum. People burp up to thirty times a day.

**No Dr. Mira.** A small "NIH" label carries the source, as "NHS" does in the tooth video.

## Video 4: Why Your Stomach Growls When You're Hungry (the visit)

**The idea:** Dr. Mira listens with a stethoscope, and we go inside through it.

1. **First frame:** a quiet room, a loud GRRR, a red face. "Hungry? Your stomach is doing the
   housework."
2. **The clinic, short:** "Doctor, why is it so loud?" She puts the stethoscope on the belly and
   the picture goes inside.
3. **Inside:** an empty stomach, and a sweeping crew with brooms pushing the last crumbs toward
   the exit in big squeezing waves. The sound echoes down the hollow tunnel like water pipes.
4. **Back to Dr. Mira:** an empty stomach is not resting, it squeezes hard to sweep itself out,
   and those same squeezes may help you feel hungry. Most tummy sounds are normal.
5. **Last frame:** lunch arrives, the crew puts the brooms down.

**Dr. Mira:** about a quarter of the video, in a new set-up (stethoscope, a different patient:
the mum, who has never appeared).
