## 1. What Happens If You Swallow Gum  (posts 8 am)

**Hooks**
- **A** "You swallow gum. Seven years inside you?"
- B "Swallow gum? Your stomach has a plan."
- C "You swallow gum. Now what happens?"

**The story**
1. Opens on the gum sitting in the stomach. The food pipe asks: "Seven years? Does gum ever come out?"
2. Rewind: the gum comes down, the stomach mixes it like the rest of his food, and it is still there.
3. That evening the boy is worried: "Mom says gum stays in my tummy for seven years!"
4. Dr. Mira: a swallowed thing that reaches the stomach will most likely travel all the way through.
5. The twist: gum can still cause trouble. Doctors reported three children with a blockage from swallowed gum. So gum is for chewing: spit it out.
6. The answer: "So yes, gum does come out, often within a week."
7. Last scene: between meals the stomach sweeps out what is left, and the gum leaves.

**Sources:** MedlinePlus (US National Library of Medicine), swallowed objects · Milov and others, Pediatrics 1998 · NIH, how the digestive system works · Goyal and others 2019, how the stomach empties.

**You should know:** no allowed source names gum, and none tests the "seven years" story. The
script answers with what MedlinePlus says about swallowed objects in general, and never says
the stomach "cannot digest" gum. The three children come from a study's summary; its full text
is not open to read.

**New in the picture:** the gum (falls in, floats, leaves through the exit). Boy and Dr. Mira in
a lavender clinic; a sunny yellow kitchen at home.

## 2. What Happens If You Skip Brushing at Night  (posts 1 pm)

**Hooks**
- **A** "Skip brushing at night, your teeth pay."
- B "You skip brushing at night. Germs feast."
- C "Skip brushing at night? Your spit is asleep." (gives the twist away early)

**The story**
1. Opens on the tooth at night, germs all over it. The germ asks: "Why is night worse than day? Watch."
2. Rewind to bedtime: he was too tired to brush. Dinner is still on the tooth, and the spit, its bodyguard, almost stops while he sleeps.
3. All night, no spit, all the acid the germs want. A chalky spot appears.
4. Many nights later, at the bathroom mirror: "Ow! Why does this tooth ache?"
5. Dr. Mira: mouth germs turn leftover food into acid, and that acid attacks the enamel.
6. The twist: by day your spit fights the acid. In sleep, it almost stops.
7. The answer: "Night is worse because your spit sleeps. Brush last thing, two minutes, then spit, don't rinse."
8. Last scene: the brush arrives at bedtime and the germs are swept off.

**Sources:** NHS, how to keep your teeth clean · Dawes 2008, Journal of the American Dental Association · NIH dental institute, tooth decay (two pages).

**You should know:** "spit almost stops in sleep" comes from a review written by one researcher.
The NHS agrees on the advice (night brushing is the important one) without giving that reason.

**New in the picture:** the tooth at night (dark mouth, moon and stars). The young man and
Dr. Mira in a pink clinic; a grey-blue bathroom at home.

## 3. Why Your Stomach Growls When You're Hungry  (posts 6 pm)

**Hooks**
- **A** "Hungry? Your stomach is doing the housework."
- B "Your hungry stomach growls like water pipes."
- C "Your stomach growls when hungry. Here's why." (safe, less curious)

**The story**
1. Opens on the empty stomach squeezing, sound rings coming off it. "Housework? So what makes that growling noise?"
2. Rewind: lunch was hours ago, the stomach is nearly empty. Strong squeezes roll through it and sweep the last crumbs out before the next meal.
3. Meanwhile, in a quiet room: "Shh! Not now, stomach!"
4. Dr. Mira: your gut is hollow. When it squeezes, the sound echoes like water pipes.
5. The twist: an empty stomach isn't resting. It squeezes hard to sweep itself out. And those same squeezes may help you feel hungry.
6. The answer: "So the growling noise is your hollow gut at work. Most tummy sounds are normal."
7. Last scene: lunch arrives and the housework is done.

**Sources:** MedlinePlus, abdominal sounds · Sanger and others 2011, "The hungry stomach" · Goyal and others 2019.

**You should know:** one source says the empty stomach squeezes to tidy up; another says tummy
sounds come from the gut moving. No single source says "the growl is the tidying" in one
sentence, so the script never says that. I changed the first hook for this reason.

**New in the picture:** the squeeze travelling along the stomach, sound rings, crumbs leaving.
The young man and Dr. Mira in a green clinic; a terracotta living room in the evening.

## 4. What Happens When You Burp  (posts 9 pm)

**Hooks**
- **A** "When you burp, a tiny door opens."
- B "When you burp, it's air you swallowed." (gives the twist away early)
- C "When you burp, your stomach lets go."

**The story**
1. Opens on the stomach stretched with air. "Guess how many times a day you burp?"
2. Rewind: he is gulping a fizzy drink. With every gulp air comes down too and piles up at the top. The stretch opens the ring of muscle, and out it goes.
3. One fizzy drink later, in the kitchen: "Burp! Oops. Excuse me!"
4. Dr. Mira: every time you swallow, you swallow a little air too.
5. The twist: a burp isn't your food talking. It's that air coming back up.
6. Fewer burps: eat slowly, go easy on fizzy drinks and gum.
7. The answer: "And it's normal. People burp up to thirty times a day."
8. Last scene: small sips, hardly any air, one small burp.

**Sources:** MedlinePlus, belching · NIH, gas in the digestive tract. Every line is straight from these two pages.

**New in the picture:** burp bubbles going up the food pipe. The boy and Dr. Mira in a
stone-grey clinic; a rose kitchen at home.
