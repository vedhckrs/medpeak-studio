## The idea in one line

One scoop of creatine goes on a **journey**: into the blood, into the muscles, where it recharges the
muscle's tiny batteries during a hard lift and pulls water in with it (the scale jumps). A little of it
breaks down and reaches the kidneys, so a blood test can look like kidney trouble. The journey ends at
the clinic, where Dr. Mira explains the scale and the blood test.

- **Story shape: `journey`** (one thing travels through several places). Rabies was a `clock`, night
  brushing a `fight`, no-sleep a `crew`, so this is new on the channel. Dr. Mira only at the end.
- **About 46 s**, then Joey's CTA end card.
- **Places (5):** the gym (hook), the blood (micro), inside a muscle cell (micro), the kidney filter
  (micro), the clinic (end).

## Care (a supplement; the topic bank's rule)

- **No dose.** No grams, no "loading", no "you should take it". The video says what happens, not how much.
- **Not "safe for everyone".** Kidney disease: ask first (E2). Blood tests: tell the doctor (E1).
- **Small, honest effect.** Branch 2003 (100 studies): small effects, best for short repeated efforts
  of 30 s or less, "does not appear to be effective in improving running and swimming". The video says "a
  bit more power", never "huge gains".
- **Industry ties, for you to know.** The ISSN papers (kreider-2017, antonio-2021) and the hair trial
  (lak-2025) have authors paid by creatine makers (stated in the papers). The three independent ones,
  branch-2003, raihan-2026 and almeida-2026, carry the claims about effect size and kidneys. I recommend keeping the ISSN
  papers only for plain biology (where creatine is stored, what it does in the cell).

## Sources (every quote read on 2026-11-21)

- **kreider-2017** Kreider RB et al. ISSN position stand: safety and efficacy of creatine supplementation.
  J Int Soc Sports Nutr 2017;14:18. PubMed 28615996 (full text PMC5469049). "The majority of creatine is
  found in skeletal muscle (~95%)"; "absorption into the blood is nearly 100%"; "the free energy released
  from the hydrolysis of PCr into Cr + Pi can be used as a buffer to resynthesize ATP ... particularly during
  maximal effort anaerobic sprint-type exercise"; "About 1-2% of intramuscular creatine is degraded into
  creatinine ... and excreted in the urine".
- **antonio-2021** Antonio J et al. Common questions and misconceptions about creatine supplementation.
  J Int Soc Sports Nutr 2021;18:13. PubMed 33557850 (PMC7871530). "the most common adverse effect of
  creatine supplementation is water retention in the early stages (first several days)"; the loading phase
  "typically results in a 1-3 kg increase in body mass, mostly attributable to net body water retention";
  "caution when using blood creatinine ... in individuals who ... supplement with creatine"; the 1998
  kidney case was a man "who had kidney disease for 8 years".
- **branch-2003** Branch JD. Effect of creatine supplementation on body composition and performance: a
  meta-analysis. Int J Sport Nutr Exerc Metab 2003;13:198-226. PubMed 12945830. 100 placebo-controlled
  studies; "Small, but significant" effects; greater for "repetitive-bout ... tasks < or = 30 s"; "does
  not appear to be effective in improving running and swimming performance"; no difference between men
  and women.
- **raihan-2026** Raihan FA, Supriyanta A. Creatine supplementation and kidney function in healthy,
  physically active adults: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Int Urol Nephrol, 17 Nov 2026.
  PubMed 43050022. 12 trials; creatine "raised serum creatinine by 12.10 µmol/L (≈ 0.14 mg/dL)"; the
  evidence "is compatible with no effect of creatine on kidney function in healthy adults but ... does not
  establish renal safety". No industry ties.
- **almeida-2026** de Souza Almeida A et al. Impact of creatine supplementation on kidney health: a
  systematic review and meta-analysis. Int Urol Nephrol 2026;58:5423-34. PubMed 42507286. 26 studies, 1036
  people; serum creatinine up "0.14 mg/dL"; no difference when filtration was measured directly (Cr-EDTA);
  "These findings likely reflect altered creatinine metabolism rather than kidney injury".
- **lak-2025** Lak M et al. Does creatine cause hair loss? A 12-week randomized controlled trial.
  J Int Soc Sports Nutr 2025. PubMed 40265319. 38 men completed; "no significant differences in DHT
  levels ... or hair growth parameters between the creatine and placebo groups".
