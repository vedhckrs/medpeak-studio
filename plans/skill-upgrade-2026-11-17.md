# MedPeak Toons: full skill upgrade plan

**Your words (17 November):** "every video looks same, repeated template, icons and all, not
interesting and visually appealing and viral, this going nothing special."

You are right, and it can be measured. This plan says what is the same, why it happened, what
changes, and how I prove the new look to you before making videos in bulk again.

Nothing in this plan is built yet. The four Day 1 videos are parked and not voiced.

## 2. Why it happened (my mistakes, not bad luck)

1. **One skeleton.** My own script checker demands the same scene order for every video. It
   was built to protect quality and it stamped out variety.
2. **I fed my "something new every 3 seconds" rule with stickers.** A sticker counts as
   "something new", so I added stickers instead of making things happen. That is why today's
   four have the most labels of all.
3. **My month plan reused one picture for many videos** to reach 3 to 4 a day. That is exactly
   what makes them look alike.
4. **Nothing physical happens.** The top Shorts in this style show fights, things breaking, a
   hand or a tool coming in, little characters with jobs. Ours show two faces talking while a
   dial moves a little. I listed these gaps on 16 November and left four of them open:
   job characters, slapstick, things reaching in from outside, and silent moments for a joke.
5. **The changes are small.** A gum the size of a coin, a few bubbles. Nothing fills the screen.

## 3. What changes: ten upgrades

### 1. Five story shapes instead of one
| Shape | What it is | Dr. Mira? |
|---|---|---|
| **The fight** | two sides battle inside the body (germs against spit, acid against a pill) | short, or not at all |
| **The journey** | we follow one thing through several places (gum: mouth, food pipe, stomach, gut) | at the end |
| **The clock** | one place over time: 1 minute, 1 hour, 8 hours, with the damage growing | no |
| **The crew at work** | the organ's workers do a job and something goes wrong | no |
| **The visit** | today's shape: home, then the clinic | yes |

Rules: the videos of one day all use different shapes. No shape twice in a row. Dr. Mira is in
at most half of the videos. Some videos end at home with no doctor, as the top Shorts often do.

### 2. Things happen; labels stop
- At most **5 text stickers** in a video (today: 16 to 22).
- A sticker no longer counts as "something new". Only an event does: something arrives,
  breaks, grows, fights, leaves.
- No floating icons in circles. If a burger matters, a burger falls into the stomach.
- No icon used twice in a video, and none of the day's videos share one.

### 3. A crew of small characters with jobs
New code-drawn characters, each with a name on it and a costume for its job. The first set:
acid drops (the stomach's cleaners), spit guards with shields, the germ gang (five different
germs, not one), air bubbles with faces, the valve as a doorman, sugar cubes, a sweeping crew.
They do the action that stickers describe today.

### 4. Slapstick and silent beats
A library of physical gags the characters can play: the dust-cloud fight, a knock-down, a chase,
a slip, a tug-of-war, falling asleep on the job, being swept away. And half a second of silence
after a gag so it lands. No joke copied from any channel.

### 5. The outside world comes in
Big real things enter the picture from outside the body: a toothbrush (this one exists), a fork,
a pour of soda, a straw, a finger, a pill, a glass of water. They are large and they move things.

### 6. One big picture in every video
Each video gets one moment that fills the screen and that nobody has seen before: the stomach
blown up like a balloon, the tooth going chalky and cracking, the night party on the tooth being
washed away. It only ever shows what the sources say.

### 7. More places, and each with its own look
- Every organ gets two or three views: a wide one, a close one on the spot where it happens,
  and its neighbour (stomach, then the gut below it). A video can move through them.
- Each organ gets its own colours and light. Not everything on dark red.
- Two videos of one day never share the same view and colours.

### 8. People and staging
- Use the family that is already drawn and never appeared: mum, grandpa, the baby. Add a teenage
  girl and an office worker. Clothes change from video to video.
- People sit, lie down, eat at a table, stand at a mirror. Not always two people standing.
- Dr. Mira gets new set-ups (at a bedside, with a clipboard, pointing at a chart) and new
  gestures. Her look stays exactly as you chose it.

### 9. The first frame is designed like a poster
The sheet will show you **three first frames** next to the three hook lines, so you choose the
opening picture as well as the words. Each must have one big subject, a strong face and
movement inside the first half second.

### 10. Sound that is not the same every time
Three or four playful music tracks that take turns, each steady like the one you picked, and
each approved by your ear before use. Each place gets its own quiet background sound.
New music may cost credits: I quote it before making any.

## 4. A new check: "too alike"

Until now nothing stopped two videos from looking the same. A new check compares every new
script with the last ten and fails it if they share too much:

- the story shape, the places and their colours
- the layout of the first frame
- the sentences ("But here's the twist" at most once in five videos; "Rewind!" the same)
- the cast, Dr. Mira's gestures and position
- the icons, the sticker count, the music

A script that fails is rewritten before you ever see it.

---

## 5. Proof before bulk: two pilots

I do not want you to approve words. I want you to see it.

**Pilot 1: "What Happens If You Skip Brushing at Night", as a fight.**
The sources stay the same. The picture changes completely:
1. First frame: a party on the tooth at night. Five germs, music notes, the tooth terrified.
2. Earlier that evening: the spit guards stand on the tooth with shields and wash the acid and sugar away.
3. Bedtime, no brush. The guards yawn, lie down and fall asleep one by one (the spit almost stops in sleep).
4. The germs creep out, throw the party, and pour acid. A chalky patch spreads.
5. Many nights later, one short scene: the ache at the bathroom mirror.
6. The next night a huge toothbrush comes in from outside like a snow plough and clears the party. The guards wake up. Brush last thing, two minutes, spit, don't rinse.

You get the old version and the new version side by side.

**Pilot 2: "What Happens If You Swallow Gum", as a journey**, so you can see that two videos
no longer look alike: mouth, food pipe, stomach, gut, out. Three places instead of one.

If you like both, I build the remaining shapes and the daily videos start again.

## 7. What does not change

Your settled choices stay as they are: 100% code-drawn, Dr. Mira's look, the boy's cap, the
tidy flat rooms, a playful music mood, no caption track, nothing in a description about how the
videos are made, a source behind every medical line, and a camera that does not jump.

One thing I would like to add to the camera, only with your yes: one slow, smooth move toward
the big picture of each video. No cuts added, nothing sudden.

## 8. What I cannot promise

"Viral" is not something anyone can promise. This plan removes the things that make people
swipe away: a picture they feel they have already seen, labels instead of action, and a story
they can predict. Whether a video takes off is then decided by the viewers, and I adjust every
week from the real numbers.
