## The idea in one line

Your brain has a **night cleaning crew**. When you sleep, the gaps between brain cells open and fluid
washes the day's waste away. Skip sleep and the waste ("Gunk") piles up, and the next day you are slow,
blinking out for seconds without knowing it. The next night he sleeps, and Gunk is washed out.

- **Story shape: `crew`** (the organ's workers do a job, and it goes wrong). Rabies was a `clock` and night
  brushing a `fight`, so this one is new. No doctor in this shape.
- **About 46 s**, then the narrator's CTA end card (Joey).
- **Places (3):** inside the brain, microscopic, three times (the hook, the middle, the end); a bedroom at
  night; the same person the next morning.

## What I will not say (care)

- **No "causes Alzheimer's".** The study measured more beta-amyloid after one night, in 20 healthy people;
  it did not show disease. The script says "more of it", nothing more.
- **"Sixty percent wider" is in mice**, and the line says so.
- **"As bad as being drunk"** is NHLBI's wording for driving and the 2000 study's for some tests, not for
  everything. The line says "some tests".
- No sleeping-pill or supplement advice. Melatonin here is the body's own signal, not a pill.

## Sources (every quote read on 2026-11-21)

- **xie-2013** Xie L et al. Sleep drives metabolite clearance from the adult brain. Science 2013;342:373-7.
  PubMed 24136970. In live mice, "natural sleep or anesthesia are associated with a 60% increase in the
  interstitial space", with a "striking increase in convective exchange of cerebrospinal fluid with
  interstitial fluid", which "increased the rate of β-amyloid clearance during sleep".
- **shokri-2018** Shokri-Kojori E et al. (NIH). β-Amyloid accumulation in the human brain after one night of
  sleep deprivation. PNAS 2018;115:4483-8. PubMed 29632177. In 20 healthy people, "one night of sleep
  deprivation, relative to baseline, resulted in a significant increase in Aβ burden in the right
  hippocampus and thalamus".
- **williamson-2000** Williamson AM, Feyer AM. Occup Environ Med 2000;57:649-55. PubMed 10984335. "After 17-19
  hours without sleep ... performance on some tests was equivalent or worse than that at a BAC of 0.05%."
- **nhlbi-effects** NHLBI (NIH), How Sleep Affects Your Health (updated 15 June 2022). Microsleep is "brief
  moments of sleep that happen when you're normally awake"; "You can't control microsleep, and you might
  not be aware of it"; sleep deficiency "harms your driving ability as much or more than being drunk".
  <https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/sleep-deprivation/health-effects>
- **nhlbi-hours** NHLBI (NIH), How Much Sleep Is Enough (updated 24 March 2022). Adults 18+: "7 to 8 hours a
  day"; "If you regularly lose sleep or choose to sleep less than needed, the sleep loss adds up."
  <https://www.nhlbi.nih.gov/health/sleep-deprivation/how-much-sleep>
- **cdc-sleep** CDC, About Sleep (15 May 2024). Adults 18-60: "7 or more hours".
  <https://www.cdc.gov/sleep/about/index.html>
