## The idea in one line

A bee's stinger spears into Kid's hand and grins up at us. Mom reaches in with tweezers and the Stinger CHEERS ("squeeze me!"),
because tweezers squeeze more poison out. Kid stops her: scrape it sideways with a card. Two days later the lump is still
there, and Dr. Mira explains what is normal, what helps, and the signs that mean call for help now.

- **Format 6, shape `visit`**: one story, two body places (the skin in cross-section with the outside world above it, then a
  close-up of the lump), one time card, then Dr. Mira's answer. About 55 s, inside the body about 65%.
- **The twist is a myth on screen**: the tweezers. The picture shows why: they would squeeze the sac.
- **The outside reaches in from above**: the skin stage shows the surface with air above it, so Mom's giant tweezers and the
  bank card come down INTO the frame while we stay inside. No cut to a room until the doctor.

## The facts (each from an allowed source, words read on 2026-11-23)

| # | Fact in the video | Source (exact words) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Do not pull a stinger out with tweezers: you could squeeze poison out | NHS: "Do not use tweezers to pull out a stinger as you could squeeze poison out of it." (CDC: "Never squeeze the stinger or use tweezers.") |
| 2 | Scrape it out sideways with a fingernail or the edge of a bank card, then wash with soap and water | NHS: "Brush or scrape the stinger sideways with your fingernail or the edge of a bank card." "Wash the area with soap and water." |
| 3 (the answer) | A small swollen lump is normal and should get better in a few days; a wrapped ice pack up to 20 minutes; keep it raised | NHS: "a small, swollen lump on the skin"; "The bite or sting should get better in a few days."; "put an ice pack wrapped in a cloth ... for up to 20 minutes"; "keep the area raised if you can" |
| care | Get emergency help at once if the lips, mouth, throat or tongue swell, or breathing or swallowing is hard | NHS (call 999 if): "suddenly has swollen lips, mouth, throat or tongue"; "struggling to swallow"; "struggling to breathe". "These can be signs of a serious allergic reaction". NHS anaphylaxis: "a life-threatening allergic reaction that happens very quickly." |

Sources: nhs.uk/conditions/insect-bites-and-stings/, nhs.uk/conditions/anaphylaxis/,
cdc.gov/niosh/outdoor-workers/about/insects-and-scorpions.html.
**Global audience**: the video says "call emergency help" (not 999); the description names 999 / 911 / 112.
**Not said** (no source says it): that honeybees always leave the stinger, that it "keeps pumping", or speed claims.

## Care

- No folk remedies (no vinegar, toothpaste, baking soda); nothing beyond the NHS and CDC words.
- The tweezers are shown as the wrong way, never as working; the card is the right way.
- The care line is the last of the answer, and the description repeats it with the emergency numbers.
