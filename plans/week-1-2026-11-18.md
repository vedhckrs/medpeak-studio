## Day 1: What Happens If You Swallow Gum
- **Hooks:** A "Swallow gum? Your stomach can't melt it." · B "You swallow gum. Seven years inside you?" · C "Swallow gum, and your acid gives up."
- **First picture:** a pink wad of gum sitting in the stomach, acid splashing off it.
- **Open question:** "So does it stay in there for years?"
- **Twist:** your stomach really cannot break gum down, but it does not have to: the gut moves it along like anything else it cannot digest.
- **Best last (the answer):** it leaves in days, not years. One piece is no harm; a mouthful at once is the rare problem, mostly in small children.
- **Sources:** Milov et al., Pediatrics 1998 (three children with blockages after swallowing gum, PubMed 9685468); MedlinePlus, swallowed foreign object (most pass within a week).
- **To build:** a piece of gum on the stomach scene (small).
- **Open point:** I have no source yet that says "gum itself leaves in days" in so many words, and none that tests the "seven years" story. If I cannot find one, the script says only what the two sources above say, and hook B is not used.

## Day 2: What Happens If You Skip Brushing at Night
- **Hooks:** A "Skip brushing at night, your teeth pay." · B "You skip brushing at night. Germs feast." · C "Your spit sleeps. Skip brushing at night?"
- **First picture:** a tooth at night, germs all over it, a chalky spot already showing.
- **Open question:** "Why is night worse than day?"
- **Twist:** it is not what you ate. Your spit, the tooth's bodyguard, slows down while you sleep, so the acid has all night.
- **Best last:** brush last thing at night with fluoride toothpaste, and spit, do not rinse.
- **Sources:** NHS, How to keep your teeth clean; reviews in PubMed Central on sleep and tooth decay (saliva flow is lower at night).
- **To build:** night on the tooth scene (small).

## Day 3: Why Your Stomach Growls When You're Hungry
- **Hooks:** A "Your stomach growls loudest when it's empty." · B "Hungry? Your stomach growls to clean itself." · C "Your stomach growls, and hungry isn't why."
- **First picture:** an empty stomach squeezing hard, sound rings coming off it.
- **Open question:** "So what is it actually doing in there?"
- **Twist:** it is not begging for food. Between meals the gut runs cleaning waves (doctors call them hunger contractions), and a hollow gut echoes like water pipes.
- **Best last:** it is cleaning up, sweeping leftovers along. Most tummy sounds are normal and need nothing done.
- **Sources:** StatPearls (NIH), Physiology, Motilin; "The hungry stomach", PubMed Central; MedlinePlus, Abdominal sounds.
- **To build:** a squeezing wave on the stomach scene (small to medium).
- **Open point:** that the cleaning waves are what you hear is my reading of two sources together; the script's wording will stay inside what each one says.

## Day 4: What Lemon Water Does to Your Teeth
- **Hooks:** A "Lemon water all day thins your teeth." · B "Lemon water is acid on your teeth." · C "Your teeth soften after every lemon water."
- **First picture:** a tooth whose enamel coat is worn thin at the edge, a lemon slice beside it.
- **Open question:** "So should you brush right after?"
- **Twist:** sugar-free does not save you: it is the acid, not the sugar. And brushing straight after makes it worse.
- **Best last:** no brushing for at least half an hour; rinse with plain water, keep sour drinks to mealtimes.
- **Sources:** NHS trust oral-health pages (acidic and sugar-free drinks erode enamel; wait before brushing); a lab study of drinks on enamel, PubMed Central.
- **To build:** enamel thinning on the tooth scene (small to medium).
- **Open point:** the NHS pages give different waiting times (30 minutes to an hour); the script will say "at least half an hour".

## Day 5: What Happens When You Drink Tea Too Hot
- **Hooks:** A "Tea too hot burns your food pipe." · B "Drink tea too hot? Your throat pays." · C "You drink tea hot. Too hot?"
- **First picture:** the food pipe scalded red as steaming tea pours down.
- **Open question:** "How hot is too hot?"
- **Twist:** it is not the tea, and not coffee either. It is the heat itself.
- **Best last:** above 65 degrees is too hot. Wait a few minutes before the first sip.
- **Sources:** World Health Organization cancer agency (IARC), press release 244, 2016: drinks above 65 °C "probably" cause cancer of the food pipe; later studies in PubMed Central.
- **To build:** the food pipe scene rebuilt (its face moves to the wall so nothing passes over it, as on the stomach) plus heat. This is the week's big build.
- **Open point:** the agency's word is "probably". The script will say exactly that and will not frighten.

## Day 6: What Happens When You Grind Your Teeth
- **Hooks:** A "Grind your teeth and they wear flat." · B "You grind your teeth in your sleep." · C "Stress makes you grind your teeth flat."
- **First picture:** two teeth grinding at night, the top one worn flat with a crack.
- **Open question:** "Why do you do it in your sleep?"
- **Twist:** you may never know you do it. It happens while you sleep, and someone else often hears it first.
- **Best last:** stress is the most common cause: wind down before bed. A dentist's night guard protects the teeth.
- **Sources:** NHS, Teeth grinding (reviewed April 2026).
- **To build:** an upper tooth and wear on the tooth scene (medium).

## Day 7: What Happens When You Burp
- **Hooks:** A "When you burp, your stomach lets go." · B "When you burp, it's air you swallowed." · C "When you burp, a tiny door opens."
- **First picture:** the stomach stretched by a bubble of air at the top, the valve bulging.
- **Open question:** "So why does it come back up?"
- **Twist:** a burp is not from your food. It is air you swallowed.
- **Best last:** the air stretches the top of the stomach, the valve relaxes and lets it out. Fewer burps: eat slowly, skip fizzy drinks and gum.
- **Sources:** MedlinePlus, Belching (reviewed July 2025); NIH NIDDK, Gas in the digestive tract.
- **To build:** nothing new (the stomach scene already shows air and the valve). The easiest day.

## How each day runs
1. Script with sources; every check must pass before you see anything.
2. Silent preview to you.
3. On your yes: voices (quoted first), render, a listen with earshot, the finished video to you.
4. On your yes: upload as private, then public and the question comment when you say so. No caption track.
