# Reference study: how the top cartoon health Shorts are built (2026-11-16)

Studied: the 20 most-viewed Shorts of a large cartoon health channel (9 to 28 million views each),
one frame per second (1,178 frames), plus cut detection and a per-second picture-change measure.
**Format study only.** Never copy their characters, drawings, jokes, titles, voices or audio.
Our characters, sets and scripts stay our own.

## The numbers: theirs against ours (before the v7 plan)
| Measure | Their 20 Shorts | Our 4 videos |
|---|---|---|
| Length | 24-97 s, median 60 s | 19-50 s |
| Cuts | one every 14.6 s (61 cuts in 1,181 s) | one every 2.0 s |
| Typical shot | 15 s; the longest holds 46 s | 2 s; the longest 7 s |
| Seconds where the picture barely changes | 85% | 0% |
| Seconds with a big change | 6% | 64% |
| Places per video | 2 to 5 | 2 |
| Words | median 158, at 2.8 words a second, with silent beats for gags | about 120, wall to wall |
| First word | median 1.3 s | 0 s |
| Loudness / music under voice | -9.4 LUFS / 9 dB under | -14 LUFS / 9 dB under |

Their camera is locked. There are no zooms, no close-ups and no camera moves. A cut happens only
when the story goes somewhere else. Everything that keeps the viewer watching happens INSIDE one
steady picture.

## What the format does (seen in all or most of the 20)
1. **The organ is the stage.** The body scene is an anatomical cross-section drawn in place (the
   stomach with the food pipe coming in and the gut below, the head cut sideways, a vessel wall,
   skin with its layers), filling the frame. The organ itself has a face on its wall and tiny arms.
   It is not a mascot standing in a room.
2. **The process is shown, not told.** Liquid rises, a pill melts and its edges drip, ulcers and
   wounds appear, a lung turns black, a toe swells red, gas clouds form, food piles up. The scene
   itself changes state as the story goes.
3. **The outside world reaches in.** A finger squeezes the pimple, a hand shaves the hair, clippers
   cut the nail, a scalpel and a probe enter the chest, a straw and a pour of soda come down.
4. **Job characters with labels.** Cells, chemicals and medicines are little characters with their
   name written on them and a costume for their job: a guard with a STOP sign, an antibiotic, sugar
   cubes, insulin with a shield, stress chemicals on fire, a hard stool as a bodybuilder.
5. **Slapstick.** Dust-cloud fights (5 videos), knock-downs, punches, a prop that breaks, a
   character asleep in a sleep mask, one that comes back muscular after the medicine works.
6. **A story, not a list.** Something happens -> it gets worse -> a time card -> the person feels it
   -> the doctor explains. Recurring family (mum, dad, son, baby). The organ hears the people
   talking outside.
7. **Time cards.** A black card with "LATER...", "FEW MOMENTS LATER", "DAY 15" (9 of 20).
8. **Label banners.** A small box naming the place ("VEIN", "OVARIES") or a part (8 of 20).
9. **Call and thought bubbles.** Another organ appears in a round bubble to talk (the brain "on the
   phone"); the doctor's advice appears as a small diagram in a thought bubble.
10. **The person at home.** A short real-world scene of the consequence: on the toilet, dizzy on
    the sofa, slapped awake for snoring, at the dinner table (10 of 20; 6 end there with no doctor).
11. **The doctor scene** (14 of 20): one steady wide picture for 10 to 33 seconds, the doctor with
    a clipboard, the patient or the whole family. About a third of the video.
12. **Captions**: one word, small, white with a dark outline, no box, about three quarters down the
    frame. They do not cover the drawing.

## What MedPeak Toons still misses
| # | Gap | Ours today | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Calm camera | a cut every 2 s, never a still second | this is the "jumping" feeling; theirs rests 85% of the time |
| 2 | The organ as the stage | mascots standing in generic body rooms | the viewer does not see inside the organ |
| 3 | Show, don't tell | characters SAY "I stick, I melt, I burn" | theirs shows the pill stick, melt and burn |
| 4 | State changes | sets only have ambient motion | no fill level, melting, damage, swelling, colour change |
| 5 | The outside reaching in | props pop up with a halo | no pour, hand, tool or food entering the body |
| 6 | Job characters with labels | organs, cells and germs only | no acid, sugar, insulin, antibiotic; no names on characters |
| 7 | Slapstick | polite gestures | no cloud fight, knock-down, breaking prop, sleep mask |
| 8 | Story with a consequence | a three-point list and a lecture | no "later", no person feeling it at home |
| 9 | Time cards, call bubbles, diagram bubbles | none | they carry the story between places |
| 10 | Home scenes and the family | office only; mum, kid, grandpa, toddler unused | half of theirs show the person at home |
| 11 | Captions | a big boxed tag over the characters | theirs are small and never hide the drawing |
| 12 | Length and silent beats | 40-50 s of non-stop talk | theirs run about 60 s and let a gag play without words |

What we already have that they do not: more polished characters, a source behind every doctor
line, a script gate, and a camera that CAN do lenses and depth when a moment needs it.

## Owner decisions this raises
- **Retention 7, point 4 (a change every 3 seconds).** The reference meets it with things happening
  inside a steady picture (an entrance, a prop, an effect, a state change, a caption), not with
  cuts. A "zoom on the same frame" is not a change; an event is.
- **Retention 7, points 2 and 3 (promise, counting).** The reference never says "three things" or
  "number one". It holds the viewer with a story. Keep the counting inside the doctor's part, or
  drop it for Toons: the owner decides.
